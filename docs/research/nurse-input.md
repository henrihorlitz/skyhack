# Domain input from a nurse (Lisbon hospital ward)

Source: Henri's girlfriend, a nurse in Portugal, written answers from Sep 2026. Use these to shape seed data and pitch claims.

## Typical daily doctor's note

A structured text on the patient's current clinical situation:

1. **Problems:** the main problems that brought the patient in, marked **active** or **resolved**.
2. **Findings:** relevant blood tests, examination results and other key clinical findings, i.e. what changed since the last note.
3. **Plan for the next day:** planned blood tests, examinations or procedures, plus **instructions for the nursing team** (e.g. draw blood, give IV fluids or other treatments, new exams scheduled).

→ Seed chart notes should follow this three-part structure.

## What nurses can and cannot communicate

**Can (general condition and wellbeing):**
- "The patient is stable."
- "He is awake and responsive."
- "His vital signs have been stable."
- "He ate well today."
- Any obvious changes in their condition

**Cannot (medical scope, handled by the doctor):**
- Diagnoses
- Examination or imaging findings
- Blood-test results or their interpretation

Questions like these are directed to the medical team.

## Family calls

- It varies by hospital and ward, but repeated calls from relatives are common.
- Illustrative example: a **22-bed ward** means ~22 families trying to reach the medical team, mostly during the **12:00–13:00** phone window, with only **~3 doctors** covering those patients.
- Some relatives call once, others 2, 3 or up to ~10 times when they can't get through.

**Pitch guidance (from the nurse):** don't claim "10 calls per family per day" as a statistic. Present repeated unanswered calls as a recurring operational problem, with this ward as an illustrative example.
